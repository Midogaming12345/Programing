5 Essential Words to Expand Your Vocabulary

Abridge

/əˈbrɪdʒ/ (uh-BRIJ)
Verb
To shorten or condense something, such as a text or story, while keeping its essential meaning.

Example Sentences:

The editor decided to abridge the novel for the audiobook version.
To save time, she abridged her speech from 20 minutes to 10.

Additional Notes

Often used in publishing; synonyms include "condense" or "summarize." Antonym: "expand." Etymology from Latin "abridgere," meaning "to shorten."

Alight

/əˈlaɪt/ (uh-LYTE)
Verb (intransitive)
To descend and settle, as from a vehicle or height; or to come down gently (e.g., a bird); can also mean to be illuminated or on fire.

Example Sentences:

The butterfly alighted on the flower petal.
Passengers alighted from the bus at the station.
The room was alight with festive decorations.

Additional Notes

Commonly used for birds or people getting off transport. Synonyms: "land" or "settle." Note the adjective form "alight" means "on fire" (e.g., the house was alight).

Hurry

/ˈhɜːri/ (HUR-ee)
Verb or Noun
To move or act quickly; a state of urgency or haste.

Example Sentences:

We need to hurry if we want to catch the train. (verb)
In her hurry to leave, she forgot her keys. (noun)

Additional Notes

Related to "hurried" (adjective) and "hurriedly" (adverb). Synonyms: "rush" or "hasten." Antonym: "dawdle." Often implies stress or impatience.

Thief

/θiːf/ (THEEF)
Noun
A person who steals, especially secretly or without permission.

Example Sentences:

The thief stole the wallet from the unattended bag.
She was caught posing as a thief in the costume party.

Additional Notes

Plural: "thieves." Synonyms: "burglar" or "robber." Related to "thievery" (noun for the act). Etymology from Old English "þeof," meaning "one who steals."

Disentangle

/ˌdɪsɪnˈtæŋɡəl/ (dis-in-TANG-guhl)
Verb
To free something from being twisted, knotted, or complicated; to separate or clarify.

Example Sentences:

It took hours to disentangle the fishing line from the weeds.
The detective worked to disentangle the facts from the rumors.

Additional Notes

Often used literally for physical knots or figuratively for problems. Synonyms: "untangle" or "unravel." Antonym: "entangle." Prefix "dis-" means "apart" or "reverse."
